    On the water Every build

    Three ways to fish, one boat

    Riley picks the style by the weather and by who is aboard, and uses Time Zero 3D ocean mapping and Furuno depth finders to find the bait. Each guest holds their own rod and a Shimano depth-counting reel.

    Mooching

    Each angler works their own rod, letting the bait down through the water to feeding salmon. No big hook-set needed. When it bites, you reel to catch up with a fish shooting to the surface.

    Trolling

    Two downriggers hold the lines at a set depth while the boat covers ground. Anglers take turns watching the rod, which suits kids, teenagers and rougher days.

    Bottom fishing

    Riley anchors on the outer coast, 200 to 700 feet deep, and sends heavy tackle and stinky bait to the ocean floor for halibut and lingcod. Once one halibut finds the scent, more follow.

    Questions guests ask

    How many people do I need?

    Both charters need four guests, and the boat holds up to six. A group of two or three can buy the boat out for the price of four.

    What is included?

    A licensed captain, rain gear, all safety gear, snacks (chips, granola bars, cookies) and water, on a 30 foot boat with an enclosed cabin and a clean bathroom. The full day also has a catered sandwich lunch. On a half day, lunch can be bought separately.

    Do I need a fishing license?

    Yes. Get a sport fishing license from Alaska Fish and Game for each day you fish, available for 1, 3, 7 and 14 days or the year. If you want to keep king salmon, you also need a King Salmon Stamp.

    Processing is not included in the rate. Riley can put you in touch with the processor at his discount, $2.95 per pound plus fish box tax and processing gratuities. Fish boxes check easily on Alaska Air and Delta.
