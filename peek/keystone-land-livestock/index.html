The ranch Every build

Four thousand seven hundred acres, moved through in turn

Keystone manages grass-fed cattle across more than 4,700 acres of grassland, oak savanna and chaparral in the Sutter Buttes, using adaptive rotational grazing. The herd moves as one through up to ten pastures, and cattle don’t return to a paddock until the grass has fully recovered.

OneTimingWhen the herd is put into a paddock.
TwoDensityHow many cattle, relative to the size of the paddock.
ThreeDurationHow long they stay in the paddock.

The herd is a mix of Murray Grey cattle, from Australia, and Bonsmara, from South Africa, a breed that does well on pasture through California’s long dry summers.

  • Grazed on Audubon Certified Bird Friendly Land

Who we are Every build

Matthew and Riley Rose-Stark

Keystone was co-founded, and is run in partnership, by Matthew and Riley Rose-Stark. They met at UC Berkeley, then spent nearly a decade learning at regenerative farms and ranches across California.

In 2022 they moved back to the ranch Riley grew up on, which had been leased out and overgrazed for decades, and started Keystone to reverse that through active, intentional management of livestock, building on her family’s ranching heritage in the Buttes.

Good to know Tended + In Bloom· from $600

Before you reserve

How do I reserve a share?

Email info@keystonelandlivestock.com with your order and the subject line Beef Share. You’re invoiced a deposit when you reserve, or you can pay the whole price of the share then.

You can reserve at any time of year.

What do I pay, and to whom?

You pay Keystone for the animal and its harvest: $1,200 for a quarter, $2,200 for a half, $3,900 for a whole. You pay the butcher directly, at pickup, for cutting, wrapping and freezing.

Because the animal is harvested on the ranch, USDA rules mean the price is set per animal rather than per pound, and the butcher is paid directly.

How much meat will I get?

An average steer of 1,100 to 1,300 lbs live weight gives about 360 to 440 lbs of take-home beef for a whole, 180 to 220 for a half and 90 to 110 for a quarter. The final weight varies with the size of the animal.

What do live, hanging and take-home weight mean?

Live weight is the animal before harvest. Hanging weight is the carcass before it is aged or processed, usually about 60% of the live weight. Take-home weight is your final packaged meat, usually 55 to 65% of the hanging weight.

When is pickup?

Pickups are in June and October in Colusa, and in late July in Fort Bragg, usually 1 to 2 weeks after harvest, once the butcher lets you know. (this year’s dates)

Can I split a quarter share?

A quarter share is the smallest size the butcher cuts. If you’d like to split one with someone, let the ranch know and they may be able to connect you with another person who wants to do the same.

What if this year’s shares are gone?

You can join the waitlist. You may be contacted if another customer cancels, or be offered a share for the following year.

How are the cattle raised?

The herd spends its whole life on pasture, eating only grass, with no grain, steroids, hormones or sub-therapeutic antibiotics.

Is the beef certified organic?

Not yet. With a herd this small, Keystone hasn’t sought organic certification for its beef. The cattle are managed in close alignment with the principles of organic agriculture, on pasture that hasn’t been treated with chemicals or fertilizer.
