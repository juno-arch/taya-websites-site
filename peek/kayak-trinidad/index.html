Big Lagoon wildlife tour

2.5 to 3 hours, daily

$119per person, all gear included

Calm, sheltered water separated from the ocean by a sandy beach, so it suits families with children. Otters, Roosevelt elk, pelicans and herons call the lagoon home, and the shore changes from sand to tule wetland to conifer forest as you paddle. Private and custom: $950 for up to eight guests, $1,900 for nine to fifteen.

Good to know Tended + In Bloom· from $600

Before your first paddle

What’s included on a tour?

On the Trinidad Bay tour: a kayak with bulkheads and perimeter lines fore and aft, a wet suit with paddle jacket, booties, a paddle, and a life jacket with a whistle. Big Lagoon tours include all paddling gear too.

What should I bring?

Synthetic or wool clothing, a waterproof camera or phone, water, a small snack, sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses and a change of clothes.

Is it okay if I’m new to kayaking?

Yes. The Trinidad Bay tour is beginner friendly and launches from the protected harbor, with basic instruction and safety first. Intro to Kayaking asks for no experience at all.

Which tour suits a family?

Big Lagoon. It’s separated from the ocean by a sandy beach, so there’s no ocean swell, which makes it a good fit for families with children. Youngest age: (your minimum age)

How far ahead should I book?

Trinidad Bay tours need advance registration, and online reservations close 24 hours before. Big Lagoon groups of up to eight register at least 24 hours ahead, and groups of nine or more 48 hours ahead. For last minute bookings, call (707) 329-0085. Rentals: a day’s notice is recommended.

When will I see whales?

In March, April and May, thousands of migrating grey whales pass Trinidad on their way north with their calves, and seeing them from a kayak is highly likely when conditions allow. The rest of the year there are birds, porpoise, seals, sea lions, otters and tide pool animals to see.
