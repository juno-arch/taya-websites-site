The museum Every build

One of only a few in the world

Triangle Tattoo & Museum is one of only a few public museums anywhere dedicated to the display of tattoo artifacts. Bring a cup of coffee and prepare to be bombarded with tattoo images that will dazzle your senses for hours.

  • Free admission
  • All ages
  • Tours on request

What’s on the walls

  • Ta mokoPortraits of Maori in New Zealand with traditional moko, from the 1800s to the present.
  • The stairwayThe American Patriotic Tattoo exhibit, with traditional designs from WWI to the present, all the way up.
  • Before electricityThe hand tools used in tattooing before the electric machine, from cultures all over the world.
  • The machinesA showcase of electric tattoo machines, from traditional coil machines to prison style rotaries.
  • Japanese tattooAntique hand tattooing instruments, portraits of the masters, and examples of their work.
  • The circus sideshowDesigns, photographs, and the retired costumes of Captain Don Leslie, one of the last of the living sideshow sword swallowers.
  • The Women’s WallAn entire wall of portraits of tattooed women, from different cultures and eras.
  • Tattoos without consentA quiet room about marks that were forced on people, so they’re remembered.

Please no photos or video in the museum, and children come with a grown-up. School and personal tours are given on request: call ahead.call ahead, or book one here.

Our story Every build

Two tattooists, one collection

Mr. G and Madame Chinchilla met in Albion in the 1980s and were soon tattooing out of a 1953 Airstream at bike runs and festivals. They’ve been collecting stories, artifacts and hand drawn flash ever since.

“Ours isn’t the biggest collection,” Mr. G says, “but we share with others what we’ve learned, and what we have.”

  1. 1986Triangle Tattoo opens on Laurel Street.
  2. 1991Onto Main Street, in one of the town’s original Victorian storefronts.
  3. 1998The Discovery Channel films eleven segments of “Tattoo! Beauty, Art & Pain” here.
  4. 2011The New York Times includes Triangle in “36 Hours on the Mendocino Coast.”
  5. Along the wayFour books, Mr. G’s columns, consulting on HBO’s Carnivàle, the National Tattoo Association’s Nicest Studio Award, and No. 17 on a list of the 101 most influential people in tattoo.

      Before you come

      Do you take walk-ins?

      Yes, walk-ins are welcome. Mr. G works by appointment, so call ahead or ask here for him.

      Does the museum cost anything?

      No, the museum is free and all ages are welcome. Donations help keep it going.

      Can I take photos in the museum?

      Please don’t. Photos and video aren’t allowed in the museum rooms, so you’ll just have to come back.

      Can kids come?

      Kids are welcome in the museum with a grown-up. Tattoos are for 18 and up, with a photo ID.

      Do you give tours?

      Yes, school and personal tours are given on request. Please call in advance.

      Capt. Don Leslie, Mr. Sideshow

      December 26, 1937 to June 4, 2007

      Sword swallower, fire-eater, human blockhead and tattooed circus man, a generous contributor to the museum, and a dear friend. One wing holds his memorabilia from 42 years of circus life: photographs, drawings and costumes, floor to ceiling.

      Online exhibits

      The rooms you could visit from anywhere.

      • Flash from the Past: Lone Wolf, London, and Ole, Copenhagen, both 1950s; a ship design from the early 1900s; a Terry Pope pin-up.

      • Old Tattooed Skin: two very old tattoos a reader wrote in about, and Mr. G’s answer. Not for the squeamish.
