Getting your web address.

This is the one piece of your website that should be bought by you, in your name — so it's truly, legally yours. It takes about fifteen minutes, costs around $12 a year, and you can do it with a cup of tea whenever you like. No rush, no tech knowledge needed — follow along and you can't go wrong.

Step one · ~5 minutes

Make yourself a Porkbun account

Porkbun (yes, really) is the friendly little shop where web addresses are sold. Go to porkbun.com, tap Sign up, and use your everyday email and a good password.

Why Porkbun? Fair prices, no upsell circus, and privacy protection included free — your home address won't be published in any public directory, which some other registrars charge extra to hide.

Step two · ~5 minutes

Buy your address

  1. In the search box, type the address we agreed on (for example yourname.com) and search.
  2. Tap + next to it, then head to the cart and check out. One year is perfect.
  3. Leave auto-renew ON — this is what keeps your address from ever accidentally expiring and being snapped up by a stranger.

Whois Privacy comes switched on by itself — nothing to do there.

Step three · ~5 minutes

Point it at your website

Now we tell your new address where your website lives. In Porkbun, find your domain and tap the little DNS button next to it. If any records are already listed, delete them (they're just parking signs). Then add these five, one at a time — tap copy here and paste over there. For each one: the Host field stays empty except the last one (put www), and everything else keeps its normal settings.

A185.199.108.153
A185.199.109.153
A185.199.110.153
A185.199.111.153
CNAMEwww → juno-arch.github.io

These point your address at the home your site lives in. If this step feels like too much, stop here and just tell me — it's genuinely fine, I can walk you through it in five minutes whenever.

Step four · the whole message

Tell me: "done!"

That's everything. Send me one message — "domain's bought and pointed" — and I'll flip the switches on my side.

Never send me your password — I don't need it, and I'll never ask for it. The address may take a few hours to wake up across the internet; that's normal, like mail forwarding kicking in. Then your site goes live at your very own address. 🌱
