What happens next

From hello to a live site.

Here’s the whole path: what I do, the few things I’ll need from you, what it costs and when. No surprises, and no homework you didn’t sign up for.

The path

The whole path, step by step

  1. A yes, in writingBefore anything starts

    I send a one-page agreement in plain words: what we’re building, the price, and these steps. You approve it by email. Half the build reserves your spot (card or bank transfer).

  2. Your content, and a 45-minute chatThe clock starts here

    We talk, and I write your words from it. You send photos, or we pick free stock photos you can keep. The build clock starts the day all your content is in. If it isn’t in 30 days after you say yes, I’ll nudge you gently; at 60 days the project closes so my calendar stays fair to everyone waiting. Your deposit covers the work done so far, and if you pick it back up within a year, it counts toward finishing. Life happens; just tell me.

  3. A draft in about a weekAbout a week after content

    You get a private link to a real, working version of your site, not a sketch. It’ll already sound like you, because it grew from our chat.

  4. Two rounds of changesBy email

    Each round is one email with everything you’d like changed, all in one place. Two rounds is usually plenty; more rounds are priced like any change ($100 an hour), and I’ll tell you the price first. If your site books or sells, we also test it all with pretend clients first: you book a fake appointment from your own phone and watch it land.

  5. LaunchThe second half is due

    Your site goes live at your own web address, and you get your one-page “Your site” sheet. The second half of the build is due at launch.

  6. 30 days of settling inTweaks on me

    Tweaks for the first 30 days are included. After that it’s care or pay as you go, whichever you chose (more on that below). Care billing starts after your 30 days of settling in, not at launch.

Your part

The few things I’ll need from you

  • Your answers and our chat The getting-started questions, then about 45 minutes of talking. I do the writing.
  • Photos of you and your work Yours or a friend’s. Good window light goes further than you’d think. Or free stock photos you can keep.
  • Changes by email One email per round, with everything in it. It keeps nothing from slipping through the cracks.
  • If you take bookings: your session types, prices, hours, and how close to a session someone may cancel Four small decisions. I set up everything else in your own booking account.
  • If you take payments: your own payment account We sign you up together in about ten minutes (the payment company may take a day or two to verify it). Your clients’ money goes straight to you, never through me.
Money, plainly

What you pay, and when

The build is paid once: half to start (it holds your spot), half at launch. Card or bank transfer.

After launch, care is $69 a month (or $690 a year) and I make your changes whenever you ask, or skip it and pay as you go: $100 an hour, and I’ll give you the price before I start. Your only other cost is your web address, about $12 a year. Just pick one by launch, because it decides where your web address lives.

  • With care I reply within 2 business days. Your web address, email from your own address and the newsletter tool (up to 1,000 subscribers) are covered. Billing starts after your 30 days of settling in, not at launch. Cancel anytime; your site stays yours.
  • Without care $100 an hour: send me one email with everything you’d like changed, and I’ll reply with a price before I start. Most small changes come to about $25 to $50. Your web address moves into your own account at launch.
  • Either way Anything big, like a new page, I’ll quote first and start once you say yes. Fixes for anything I got wrong are always free.

Founding clients get half off the build, and care locked at $49 a month (or $490 a year). Hosting is free, forever. If that ever changed, I’d move your site for free.

The handover

What lands in your hands

  • Your live site, at your own web address Without care, the address moves into your own Porkbun account at launch. With care, I hold it for you, with you listed as the legal owner.
  • Your one-page “Your site” sheet What lives where, which logins are yours, your exact colors and fonts (so flyers and cards can match), and how to reach me.
  • A login to your own booking account, if you have booking Change your hours, block off a vacation, see your week, all from your phone.
  • Every account that should be yours, in your name Payments and booking always live in your own accounts, with the passwords in your hands.
  • The site itself It’s yours. That’s the deal, in writing.
And breathe

What you never have to do

  • Learn code, hosting, or any tech vocabulary
  • Write about yourself from a blank page
  • Keep the machinery running
  • Wonder what’s happening. You can always just ask me, in plain words

You don’t need to decide anything technical. Tell me how you work, and it’ll be built around that.
